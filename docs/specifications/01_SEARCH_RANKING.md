# Search Ranking Specification

## Purpose

This document defines how search results are ranked in DinLinks.

Its purpose is to ensure that search results are consistent, fair, predictable, and trustworthy.

This specification complements the Search Experience Standard.

---

# Primary Goal

The objective of search is not to return the largest number of results.

The objective is to help users find the right business as quickly as possible.

---

# Core Principle

Search ranking is based on relevance.

Commercial relationships must never override relevance.

Users should always believe that the best result appears because it best matches their search.

---

# Ranking Priority

Search results should be ranked using the following priority order.

1. Exact Business Name Match
2. Overall Relevance
3. Verification Status
4. Profile Completeness
5. Location Relevance
6. Additional Ranking Signals

---

# Exact Business Name Match

An exact business name match always receives the highest priority.

Example:

Search:

"DinLinks"

Results:

1. DinLinks
2. DinLinks Oslo
3. DinLinks Consulting

The exact match should always appear first.

---

# Overall Relevance

When no exact match exists, ranking should prioritize businesses that best match the user's intent.

Relevance may consider:

- Business name
- Category
- Services
- Keywords
- Business description

The goal is to match what the user is actually looking for.

---

# Verification Status

Verification increases trust.

When two businesses are similarly relevant, the verified business should rank higher.

Verification should never allow an irrelevant business to outrank a more relevant one.

---

# Profile Completeness

Complete profiles provide better information for users.

When two businesses are otherwise equal, the more complete profile should rank higher.

Profile completeness should never outweigh relevance.

---

# Location Relevance

Location should influence ranking only when location is relevant to the search.

Examples:

- User searches for a city.
- User allows location access.
- User searches for nearby businesses.

Location should not affect ranking for purely informational searches.

---

# Reviews

Customer reviews may become a future ranking signal.

Until the review system is sufficiently mature and trustworthy, reviews must not significantly influence search ranking.

---

# Paid Plans

Paid subscriptions must never directly improve search ranking.

Subscription plans may unlock additional profile features, but they must not reduce search fairness.

---

# Tie-Breaking

If two businesses remain equal after applying the ranking signals, additional tie-breaking signals may be used.

Examples:

- Information quality
- Freshness of profile
- Business activity

Tie-breaking should remain predictable and consistent.

---

# Future Signals

Future ranking signals may include:

- Review quality
- User engagement
- Business responsiveness
- Data quality improvements

New ranking signals must always preserve fairness and user trust.

---

# Final Principle

Users should trust that search results are ranked because they are the most relevant—not because they are the most profitable for the platform.
